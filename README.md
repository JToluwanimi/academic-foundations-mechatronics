## 4. Why Mechatronics

I chose Mechatronics Engineering because I couldn't choose one thing to craft and build intelligent systems and structures. Mechanical design alone couldn't answer the questions I had. Electrical engineering alone couldn't answer them. Software alone couldn't either. But put all three together, and suddenly you're building systems that sense, think, and act in the physical world. That's what I wanted to do.

My first real encounter with the discipline was on TV watching what was at the time fictional realities. It was remarkable. But the fact that a machine could look at the world and decide what to do next was electrifying. I signed up for everything after that: Arduino workshops, drone assembly sessions, control systems electives, neural network courses. I wasn't the strongest programmer in my cohort. I wasn't the best welder either. But I was willing to sit with a project longer than most, and I learned that engineering rewards patience more than talent.

By the time I finished my degree, I had assembled two drones, integrated an electric vehicle dashboard, studied commercial building automation for a semester, and written a final-year thesis on delivery drones for lightweight packages. I graduated First Class. But the credential isn't the point. The point is that I now know how to look at a broken system — mechanical, electrical, software, or human — and figure out where the failure is hiding.

I am not yet the engineer I want to be. I have not worked at scale. I have not led a production system. I have not yet deployed software to thousands of users. I am early in my career, and I know it. But I am building the discipline required for that trajectory, one project at a time. This portfolio is my attempt to show that honestly — not the polished version, not the aspirational version, but the real, messy, progressing version.
